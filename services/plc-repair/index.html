Common Faults & Micro-Diagnostic Solutions

Failure Symptom Hardware Failure Point Lab Restoration Method
No 24VDC OK Light / Dead Rack Blown primary flyback controller IC, open fuse, shorted Schottky rectifier. Rebuild SMPS power stage, replace low-ESR high-temp capacitors.
Input LED ON but CPU Reads LOW Punctured input optocoupler phototransistor from external 24V spike. Desolder damaged optocoupler, install high-speed industrial isolators.
Output Relay Stuck ON or Open Welded contacts from inductive coil back-EMF, broken flyback clamp diode. Replace sub-miniature sealed relays, install fresh freewheeling clamp diodes.
Analog Input Drift / Erratic Counts Damaged operational amplifier input stage or 16-bit ADC reference chip. Replace precision op-amps, calibrate analog zero and span voltage ranges.

Our Bench Repair & Rig Testing Procedure

  1. DC Bus & Logic Rail Inspection: Verification of 3.3V, 5V, and 24V rails with thermal camera to spot micro-shorts before logic board energization.
  2. Micro-Component De-Soldering: Component-level replacement of surface-mount optocouplers, relay banks, and transceiver ICs using temperature-regulated hot-air stations.
  3. Loop Simulation Testing: Forcing all inputs with precision signal generators and verifying output relay firing under inductive test loads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need our PLC logic program to repair the hardware?

No. We test PLCs at the physical component layer (power supplies, optocouplers, input signal filters, relay contacts) without needing your confidential ladder logic.

Can you repair obsolete PLC cards that the manufacturer no longer supports?

Yes. Component-level repair is especially valuable for legacy series like Siemens S7-300 or Allen-Bradley SLC 500 where replacement cards are discontinued.
